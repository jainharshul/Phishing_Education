Why do Attackers do Email Phishing?

Attackers send phishing emails for one simple reason: it works and it pays. Unlike other cyberattacks that require deep technical skill to break through digital walls, phishing is a con artist's game that tricks people into opening the door for them. The primary driver behind all this activity is financial gain, making it a low-risk, high-reward business for criminals.

One of the most common goals is credential theft. This means stealing your usernames and passwords. An attacker might send a fake email from your bank, your email provider, or a social media site. When you log into their fake website, they capture your credentials. With these login details, they can drain your bank account, use your identity for other scams, or sell the stolen credentials to other criminals on the dark web. Your password is a key to your digital life, and phishers are trying to steal your keys.

A more direct goal is financial theft. Some phishing emails skip the step of selling your information and instead try to get you to send money directly. This can look like an invoice for a service you never used, a fake plea for help from a "friend" in trouble, or a sophisticated Business Email Compromise (BEC) scam where an attacker impersonates a company executive to trick an employee into wiring thousands of dollars to a fraudulent account. The money transfer is immediate and often irreversible.

Another major goal is malware installation. Instead of asking for information, these phishing emails contain a malicious link or attachment. The "hook" might be a fake shipping notification, a resume from a "job applicant," or a fake document you "must review." Clicking the link or opening the file secretly installs software like ransomware, which locks your files until you pay a fee, or spyware, which silently monitors everything you do to steal even more information. This turns your computer into a tool for the attacker.

For some attackers, the goal is not just money from individuals, but corporate espionage. They use highly targeted spear-phishing emails to gain a foothold inside a company. Once inside, they can steal confidential plans, intellectual property, or private customer data. This stolen information can be sold to competitors or used for blackmail, causing massive damage to the targeted business far beyond a single stolen password.

Ultimately, phishing is a multi-billion dollar criminal industry because it is cheap, easy to scale, and effective. An attacker can send a million emails for a very low cost. If even a handful of people fall for the scam, the financial return can be enormous. This combination of low effort and high potential profit is why we see so many phishing emails in our inboxes every single day.
