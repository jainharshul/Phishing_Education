Finding Links in the Raw Source

When you look at a phishing email in your inbox, a link might look safe because it says "Click Here to Secure Your Account." However, the real, dangerous destination is hidden in the email's code. To see the truth, you need to examine the link in the email's raw source, where you can't be tricked by the visible text.

In the raw source, links are written in a code called HTML. They look like this: <a href="http://malicious-website.com">Click Here to Secure Your Account</a>. The part you see in your email is the "Click Here" text. The important part is the href= value inside the quotation marks. This is the actual web address you will go to if you click. A phisher can make the visible text look completely legitimate while the href points to a dangerous fake website.

Phishers also often use link shortening services like Bitly to hide their malicious addresses. A link might just show as "bit.ly/abc123," which gives you no clue where it leads. To reveal the true destination, you can use a URL expander website. A simple trick for Bitly links is to just add a plus sign (+) to the end of the URL in your browser's address bar. For example, if you type bit.ly/xyz+ and press enter, it will take you to a Bitly page that shows you the full, real website address before you visit it. This allows you to check if the link is safe without ever clicking on it.
