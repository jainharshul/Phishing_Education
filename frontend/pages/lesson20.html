Hover links before clicking

One of the simplest and most powerful habits you can develop to avoid phishing is to hover your mouse cursor over any link in an email before you click it. When you hover, the true destination web address will appear in a small box, usually at the bottom corner of your web browser or email client. This allows you to see where the link actually goes, regardless of what the clickable text says.

This is your best defense against deception. A link might show the text "Click here to visit your bank" but when you hover over it, the pop-up might reveal the true address is "http://bak-safety.ru/login.php." The visible text is just a label, but the underlying link is what matters. Hovering reveals the truth and lets you spot fake domains or suspicious addresses.

Clicking a link in a phishing email is extremely dangerous. It can take you to a fake login page designed to steal your username and password the moment you type them in. It can also silently download malware onto your computer, giving attackers control or locking your files for ransom. By taking one second to hover and verify the link, you can prevent these serious consequences and stop an attack before it even starts.
