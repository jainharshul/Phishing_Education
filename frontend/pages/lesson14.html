Email Authentication Protocols

Email services use special security systems called authentication protocols to verify who sent an email. These systems work like digital ID checks to help stop phishing. The three main protocols are SPF, DKIM, and DMARC, and they each play a different role in checking an email's legitimacy.

SPF, or Sender Policy Framework, acts like a bouncer checking a guest list. It looks to see if the server that sent the email is officially allowed to send mail for the domain in the "From" address. If the server isn't on the list, the SPF check fails. DKIM, or DomainKeys Identified Mail, is like a tamper-proof seal on a medication bottle. It uses a digital signature to prove that the email wasn't changed on its way to you. If the seal is broken, the DKIM check fails.

DMARC, or Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting & Conformance, is the manager that makes the final decision. It looks at the results of the SPF and DKIM checks. It then tells the receiving email server what to do with a message that fails—whether to deliver it, send it to junk mail, or reject it completely.

You can see the results of these checks yourself by looking at the email's raw source code. Look for a header called "Authentication-Results." In a real email from a trusted company like PayPal, you will see results like "spf=pass" and "dkim=pass." In a phishing email, you will often see "spf=fail" or "dkim=fail," which is a clear sign the email is fake and failed its security checks.
