Grammar and Tone

For a long time, one of the easiest ways to spot a phishing email was through poor grammar, awkward phrasing, and a strange tone. These mistakes happened because many scammers were not native English speakers or did not carefully craft their messages. You might see unusual phrasing like "Kindly verify your account details at soonest" or "We have detect suspicious activité on you're profile."

However, this red flag is rapidly becoming less reliable due to new artificial intelligence (AI) tools. Advanced language models can now generate phishing emails with perfect grammar, spelling, and professional-sounding language. A scam email can now be indistinguishable from one written by a real company's communications team.

Because of this change, you should not trust an email just because it looks well-written. While bad grammar is still a clear warning sign, its absence no longer guarantees an email is safe. The most sophisticated phishing attacks today use flawless language to build trust. This means you must rely on other clues, like checking the sender's actual email address, hovering over links, and being suspicious of any unexpected request, regardless of how professionally it is written.
