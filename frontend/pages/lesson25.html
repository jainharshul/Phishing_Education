Incident Response

Realizing you have fallen for a phishing scam can be frightening, but it is critical to act quickly and calmly. The steps you take immediately after can contain the damage and protect your accounts and your organization. Having a simple plan in mind will help you respond effectively instead of panicking.

Your first action should be to disconnect your device from the network. This means turning off Wi-Fi and unplugging the Ethernet cable. This immediate isolation can prevent malware from spreading to other devices on the network or stop an attacker who may have gained access to your computer from controlling it remotely.

Next, you must secure your accounts. Change the password for the account that was compromised immediately. If you use that same password for any other websites, change those passwords as well. You should then report the incident to your IT or security team at work, or to the appropriate support if it's a personal account. They can monitor for suspicious activity and help you recover. Finally, run a full virus scan with your security software to check for and remove any malicious programs that may have been installed. Acting fast limits the attacker's opportunity to cause harm.
