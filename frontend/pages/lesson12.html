How to Read Headers

Once you have the raw source of an email, the most important part to look at is the "Received" headers. These headers act like a passport, stamping every stop the email made on its journey to your inbox. By reading them, you can trace the email's path and often discover its true origin, which is vital for spotting a fake.

The key rule for reading "Received" headers is to read them from the bottom to the top. The email journey starts at the bottom header, which is the oldest, and each new server that handles the email adds a new "Received" line above the previous one. The very last "Received" header at the top shows the final step from your own email provider to your inbox.

To find the originating IP address, look at the very first "Received" header at the bottom of the list. This header usually contains the IP address of the server that first sent the email into the internet. Finding this IP is a major clue. You can then search for this IP address using a simple "What is my IP" website or a "whois lookup" tool online. This will show you the geographic location and the internet provider associated with that IP.

This helps you answer a critical question: does the originating IP match the claimed sender? If an email pretends to be from your US-based bank, but the bottom "Received" header shows an IP address from a different country, you know it is a phishing email. Legitimate emails from large providers like Gmail or Outlook will have a chain of "Received" headers that all point back to their own trusted servers, making the path consistent and verifiable. An inconsistent or suspicious origin is a clear sign of fraud.
