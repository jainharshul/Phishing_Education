What is Phishing?

Email phishing is the process of an attacker sending a malicious email with the intention of tricking the victim into giving up valuable information or to install malware. It's not a complex computer hack, it's a trick to make you disable your existing security.

Phishing is like dressing up as someone you know and asking you to open a locked door. It's up to you to open the door and phishing happens when they convince you to open it.

Phishing is much more focused on social engineering than technical knowledge when compared to other methods of stealing private information online, which makes it far easier for those with little technical knowledge to create sophisticated phishing attacks on mass. Every day millions of phishing emails are sent and every year hundreds of thousands of people fall for a phishing email.

Phishing is a form of social engineering which uses tricks and psychology on people to get what the attacker wants. A classic example is someone calling you on the phone and pretending to be from your bank's fraud department. They create a sense of panic to get you to reveal your account number. Phishing is simply social engineering that happens through digital messages, most commonly, email.

Phishing emails are made of three parts: The "bait" which is the tempting or scary email designed to panic the victim into acting irrationally. The "hook" is hidden inside the email and is what the attacker wants the victim to do. The "catch" is if you take the bait you get hooked and the attacker gets what they want.

Email phishing generally follows a similar pattern. First, you will get an unexpected message. Then, this message will try to make you feel a strong emotion like fear, urgency, or curiosity to make you act rashly. Finally, it will ask you to do something like click a link or send information. While there are many forms of email phishing this is the most common framework they will follow.
