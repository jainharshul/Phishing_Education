Attachments and Links

Clicking on a link or opening an attachment in a phishing email is the action that gives the attacker what they want. Understanding what happens next is key to understanding the danger. A simple click can lead to two main types of attacks: credential theft or malware installation.

When you click a link, you are often taken to a fake landing page. This page is a perfect copy of a real login screen for a service like your email, bank, or company network. If you type in your username and password, you are not logging into your real account. You are directly handing your credentials to the attacker. They then use your stolen login to access your real accounts.

When you open a malicious attachment, you are often installing software on your computer without knowing it. The file could be a dangerous executable (.exe) or a document with malicious code (.docm). This software, called malware, can do many harmful things. It can lock your files and demand a ransom (ransomware), record your keystrokes to steal other passwords (spyware), or simply give the attacker remote control over your machine. Always treat unexpected attachments with extreme caution.
