From and Reply To

When you look at an email, you naturally trust the "From" address to tell you who sent it. However, phishers exploit this trust by manipulating another, less obvious field: the "Reply-To" address. Understanding the difference between these two fields is a critical defense against being tricked.

The "From" address is what you see in your inbox. It's the name and email address that appears as the sender. A phisher can easily make this look legitimate, such as "support@yourbank.com." This is the illusion they want you to believe. However, this does not always control where your reply goes.

The "Reply-To" address is a separate, hidden instruction that tells your email program where to send your response. When you hit "Reply," your email client automatically uses the "Reply-To" address instead of the "From" address. A phisher can set a "From" address that looks real, but set the "Reply-To" address to their own personal, fraudulent email account.

This trick is how many Business Email Compromise (BEC) scams work. You might get an email that appears to be from your CEO, with a "From" address that looks correct. The email asks you a question. When you hit "Reply," you assume you are emailing your CEO back. But if the phisher has set a different "Reply-To" address, your response, which may contain confidential information, goes directly to the attacker instead. Always double-check the email address in the "To" field when you hit reply to ensure your response is going to the right person.
