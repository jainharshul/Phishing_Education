Email Raw Source

To truly investigate a suspicious email, you need to look at its "raw source." This is the complete, technical version of the email, exactly as it was received by your mail server. It contains all the hidden details that you don't see in the normal inbox view. Learning to access this information is a key skill for spotting sophisticated fakes.

The way you find the raw source is different for each email program. In Gmail, you open the email, click the three dots next to the reply button, and select "Show original." In Outlook on your desktop, you open the email in its own window, go to File, then Properties, and look in the "Internet headers" box. In Apple Mail, you select the email and then choose View, then Message, then Raw Source from the top menu.

When you open the raw source, you will see a lot of text. This includes the email's "headers," which are like a travel log, and the "body," which is the message content in its raw code form. The headers show the path the email took and who sent it. You don't need to understand everything, just a few key fields.

Look for important fields like "From," "To," and "Subject." Also, look for the "Return-Path," which can be different from the "From" address and is a red flag. The most important part is the "Received" fields. You read these from the bottom to the top. The very bottom "Received" line shows the email's first step onto the internet, which can reveal its true origin and help you see if it's from a legitimate source or a fake one.
