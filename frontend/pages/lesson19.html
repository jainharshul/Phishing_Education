Spoofing & Impersonation

A core tactic in phishing is spoofing, which means faking the sender's information to look like someone you trust. Attackers primarily use two methods to do this: display name spoofing and domain spoofing. Understanding both is crucial for spotting fakes.

Display name spoofing is the simplest trick. The attacker sets the "From" field's display name to a trusted entity, like "Microsoft Support" or your boss's name. However, if you look at the actual email address next to it, it will be something completely different, like service123@gmail.com. Always look at the full email address, not just the friendly name.

Domain spoofing is more advanced. Here, the attacker forges the actual "From" address to look like it's coming from a real company's domain. They can't actually send from the real domain, but they can make it appear that way in your inbox. This is why you must carefully check for subtle misspellings in the domain name itself. For example, a phisher might use paypa1.com (with the number '1') instead of the real paypal.com, or micr0soft.com (with a zero) instead of microsoft.com. Your brain might read it correctly, but the computer sees a completely different, fake website.
