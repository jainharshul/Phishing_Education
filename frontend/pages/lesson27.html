Multi-Factor Authentication

Multi-Factor Authentication, or MFA, is one of the most powerful tools you have to protect your online accounts from phishing. It acts as a critical second lock on your digital door. Even if a phisher successfully steals your username and password, MFA can stop them from getting into your account.

The concept is simple: MFA requires you to provide two or more pieces of evidence to log in. These pieces fall into three categories: something you know (like a password), something you have (like your phone), and something you are (like your fingerprint). After entering your correct password, you are prompted for a second step, such as entering a temporary code sent to your phone or generated by an app.

This is why MFA is so effective against phishing. A phisher can trick you into giving up your password, but they are unlikely to also have your physical phone or your fingerprint. Without that second factor, the stolen password is useless on its own. Enabling MFA on your important accounts—like email, banking, and social media—ensures that a single mistake with a phishing email doesn't lead to a compromised account. It is the most important step you can take to add a layer of security that phishers cannot easily break.
