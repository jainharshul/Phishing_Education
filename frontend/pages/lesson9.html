SMS based phishing

While our focus is on email, it's crucial to know that phishing attacks happen on other platforms, too. A common method is SMS Phishing, often called "Smishing." This is simply a phishing attack sent via text message instead of email. The same psychological tricks used in emails—urgency, fear, and curiosity—are packed into a short text.

A smishing message might appear to be from your bank ("We've detected suspicious activity on your account. Reply Y to confirm your identity."), a package delivery service ("Your package is held at the depot. Confirm your address here: [malicious link]"), or even a friend. Because text messages feel more immediate and personal, and because we see them on our locked phone screens, they can sometimes be even more effective at triggering a quick, unthinking reaction.

The goal is the same: to trick you into clicking a malicious link that steals your credentials or to reply with personal information. Always be skeptical of unexpected text messages, especially those containing links. If you are unsure, contact the organization directly using a phone number from their official website, not the one provided in the text.
